Sixty-two governments sent delegations to Songdo ConvensiA, the convention center on reclaimed land west of Seoul, for the sixth IAEA International Ministerial Conference on Nuclear Power. That is the closing-day count from Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT), released on October 29, 2026, when the three-day meeting ended. It was the first time the series, which began in Paris in 2005, came to Korea.

We published a preview of this conference on October 18. This version replaces it with the outcome as of October 30, 2026, and scores the five things we said to watch.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Attendance at close: government delegations from 62 IAEA member states, 5 international organizations and about 85 companies, more than 800 people in all (MSIT, October 29). The opening-day release had said 60 countries and 8 organizations.
> - The formal outcome is a Conference President's Statement, but as of October 30 we could not find its text on the IAEA website. MSIT's closing summary lists new build, financing, regulatory cooperation, SMRs and maritime nuclear, and AI as the main threads.
> - Korea's Deputy Prime Minister Bae Kyung-hoon presided and set out three policy priorities. Side events produced a Korea-US industry dialogue and a Korea-UK research memorandum. IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi canceled his trip and spoke by video.

## 1. What came out of Incheon

### Attendance

MSIT's closing release counted government representatives, including ministers, from 62 IAEA member states, plus 5 international organizations and staff from about 85 Korean and foreign companies. Total attendance topped 800. On opening day the same ministry had said 60 countries and 8 organizations, so the two releases do not match. The closing figure is the later one.

### The president's statement

The IAEA announcement said the closing plenary would hear a Conference President's Statement, and Bae Kyung-hoon, Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Science and ICT, presided (MSIT, October 27). As of October 30 we could not find the statement's text on the IAEA website, and MSIT's closing release does not mention it. What we have instead is MSIT's own summary of the discussions:

- **New build**. Policy consistency, an independent and competent regulator, and predictable cost and schedule were presented as the core conditions. Newcomer countries were urged to put institutions and people in place early.
- **Financing**. Risk sharing among governments, companies and investors, and linking IAEA readiness reviews to support from lenders such as the World Bank Group.
- **Regulatory cooperation**. Sharing safety-review experience to cut duplication, while respecting each country's laws and regulatory authority.
- **SMRs and maritime**. Demonstration of SMRs and advanced reactors, fuel supply, spent fuel management, and the legal groundwork for maritime, offshore and remote uses.
- **AI in nuclear facilities**. Cases of AI for inspection support and knowledge management were shared. Responsibility for safety decisions stays with people.

Every item on the IAEA's original agenda shows up somewhere in that list. Whether each made it into the president's wording is a separate question, and only the text can answer it.

### What Korea put on the table

Bae's opening keynote set out three priorities (MSIT, October 27).

- **Trust built on safety and transparency**. Independent regulation, acting on IAEA peer-review recommendations, and publishing operating and safety information.
- **Safe SMR development and demonstration**. Designs aimed at AI data center power, industrial heat and ship propulsion, backed by a new SMR special act and a set of seven "SEED" projects that pool public and private effort.
- **A global production hub**. Using Korea's manufacturing and construction base for quality control and AI- and robot-assisted safety, with cooperation across operation, decommissioning, waste and advanced fuel.

Korea also ran its own session, "A New Era of Nuclear Energy," on bringing digital twins and AI monitoring into plants within existing safety requirements, and staffed a K-Nuclear exhibition hall (MSIT, October 29).

### Side meetings and memoranda

- **Korea-US industry dialogue**. On October 26, the day before the opening, MSIT and the US Embassy in Seoul brought together about 150 people from both governments and 50 companies and institutions to discuss design, construction, manufacturing, investment and fuel. The US firms included TerraPower, NuScale Power, Oklo, Kairos Power and Centrus Energy (MSIT, October 27).
- **Korea-UK research memorandum**. On October 27 KAERI (Korea Atomic Energy Research Institute) and the UK National Nuclear Laboratory signed a memorandum on civil nuclear R&D covering light-water SMRs, Generation IV systems, the fuel cycle, waste and decommissioning, and component testing and qualification. MSIT and the UK Department for Energy Security and Net Zero held a bilateral meeting beforehand, according to Yonhap News Agency.
- As of October 30 we found no reports of government-to-government memoranda, or of ministerial meetings with the Czech Republic, the UAE or Vietnam.

### Grossi's absence

Grossi said on October 25, two days before the opening, that he could not attend, and his opening speech and a joint press conference with Bae were dropped (SBS Biz and Yonhap, citing MSIT, October 26). The IAEA gave no reason in advance. According to Korean business daily Asia Business Daily, Grossi explained in his video message that he had been scheduled to brief the UN Security Council; Korean daily Electronic Times also cited a Security Council commitment.

In his video address Grossi said the IAEA's low-case projection for 2050 now exceeds 640 GW and its high case exceeds 1,000 GW, whereas the 2022 low case had shown essentially flat capacity (Korea JoongAng Daily, carrying Yonhap, October 27). The tone has moved a long way in four years.

## 2. Scoring the five things to watch

Our preview listed five things to watch. Here is how each turned out, as of October 30, 2026.

1. **Who read Korea's statement**. A deputy prime minister, one rank above a line minister, presided and gave the keynote. The message was the three priorities plus the SMR special act and the seven SEED projects; we saw no new i-SMR or export figures in coverage. Whether a separate national statement was published: not confirmed.
2. **The headcount**. 62 countries and 800-plus people at close. That is fewer countries than Washington 2022 (69) and roughly the same number of people. Russian and Chinese attendance: not confirmed.
3. **New wording in the president's statement**. Text not found. MSIT's summary covers lender linkage, regulatory cooperation, maritime and remote uses, and AI, but that is a ministry summary, not the statement.
4. **Bilateral meetings and memoranda**. The US through the industry dialogue (October 26) and the UK through the KAERI memorandum (October 27). Czech Republic, UAE, Vietnam: not confirmed.
5. **The calendar outside the hall**. On October 27, the conference's opening day, NSSC (Nuclear Safety and Security Commission, Korea's regulator) approved continued operation of Kori 3 and 4 by a 5-2 vote, according to Korean daily Kyunghyang Shinmun. Wolsong 2's operating license expires on November 1, according to Korean daily Maeil Shinmun (October 15).

## 3. What the conference was

The IAEA holds this meeting every four to five years. It is a policy forum, not a technical one. The IAEA organized it with the OECD Nuclear Energy Agency, and Korea hosted it through MSIT, the ministry that funds nuclear R&D and the i-SMR program (IAEA announcement CN-348).

- **Plenary of ministerial statements**. Ministers spoke in an order set by a ballot at IAEA headquarters in Vienna.
- **Panels and In-Focus sessions**. Five panel themes: new builds and embarking countries; regulation, including harmonization and non-proliferation; advanced reactors, SMRs and fusion; workforce and supply chains; and nuclear for maritime, offshore and remote sites. In-Focus sessions covered financing, stakeholder engagement and AI.
- **Closing plenary**. The slot for the president's statement.

One small change was in the name. The five earlier meetings all carried "21st Century" in their titles; the 2026 one did not. I read that as a sign the question has moved from whether nuclear belongs in this century to how fast it can be built.

Korea's hosting was announced at the 69th IAEA General Conference in September 2025 by Koo Hyuk-chae, MSIT's first vice minister (MSIT press release, September 17, 2025). At the 70th General Conference in September 2026 he asked counterparts in bilateral meetings to send high-level delegations (MSIT press release, September 16, 2026).

## 4. How Incheon compares

![Timeline of the six IAEA ministerial conferences on nuclear power from Paris 2005 to Incheon 2026 with participation figures including 62 countries and 800-plus at Incheon, and four boxes with the Incheon dates and attendance, the 413 operating reactors worldwide, the five panel themes and the president's statement as the outcome](/images/iaea-incheon-ministerial-2026-en.svg)

Participation figures come from the IAEA's final and president's statements and annual reports, Nuclear Engineering International and, for 2026, MSIT's closing release of October 29. Counting methods differ, so treat them as rough.

| Conference | Participation | Backdrop |
|---|---|---|
| Paris 2005 | 74 states, 10 organizations | Early talk of a nuclear revival |
| Beijing 2009 | 61 states, 808 participants | Financial crisis, China's build-out |
| St. Petersburg 2013 | 87 countries, 7 organizations, 500-plus | Two years after Fukushima |
| Abu Dhabi 2017 | 68 states, 6 organizations, about 700 | Barakah under construction |
| Washington 2022 | 69 countries, 9 organizations, about 800 | Energy crisis; Russia excluded |
| Incheon 2026 | 62 countries, 5 organizations, 800-plus | SMRs and exports; DG by video |

All five earlier meetings ended with a concluding statement by the president rather than a signed text. In 2013 the IAEA summarized the outcome as nuclear power remaining an important option for many countries. In 2022 the core point was that participants agreed nuclear power could contribute significantly to reaching net zero by 2050. Nobody voted.

The backdrop always followed the host. Abu Dhabi met while Barakah was being built; Washington met eight months into the war in Ukraine, with Russia excluded (Nuclear Engineering International, October 31, 2022). Incheon's backdrop was SMRs and manufacturing, as expected.

## 5. What was decided, and what was not

**What was decided**. The sentences of the president's statement, once published. With a Korean deputy prime minister in the chair, Korea may well have shaped the draft. Only the text will show how much.

**What was not**. Almost everything else. The i-SMR review, filed on February 27, 2026, with a full review plan reported to the regulator on August 27, runs on the clock of NSSC and KINS (Korea Institute of Nuclear Safety, its technical arm); see [our October licensing tracker](/en/posts/ismr-licensing-tracker-2026-10/) and [our baseline post on Korea's SMR program](/en/posts/korean-smr-ismr-status/). The site and operator for the US-Korea package (six AP1000 units and up to two APR1400 units, up to $120 billion, non-binding) will be settled in commercial talks, as laid out in [our export tracker](/en/posts/korea-nuclear-exports/) and [the package explainer](/en/posts/us-korea-nuclear-package/).

The homework outside the hall is also unchanged. Kori 3 and 4 have their approval but KHNP (Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power, the state utility) is aiming to restart them in the first half of 2027, and Hanbit 1 and 2 are still under review, as tracked in [our idle-reactor post](/en/posts/idle-reactors-license-bottleneck/). The $350 million Westinghouse technology fee on Dukovany and the KRW 1.2 trillion (about $890 million) El Dabaa loss provision, both raised in the October audit, were not on the conference agenda.

In the preview I said I would read Korea's page more closely than the president's. Having read it, the phrase that stuck was "production hub" rather than any new number. Whether that turns into orders will be shown by contracts, not by this conference.

**Where this goes next**. We will add the president's statement when the IAEA posts it. The next dated items in Korea are the Wolsong 2 license expiry on November 1 and the public deliberation recommendations on new reactors, which the government has said it expects in December for the 12th Basic Plan for electricity supply.
