Last month [this tracker](/en/posts/ismr-licensing-tracker-2026-10/) listed five things to watch over the next 30 to 60 days. One moved visibly. One happened but sits on a different layer from licensing, and I could not confirm any change on the other three.

The more useful news came from outside the list: a Nuclear Safety Act change that takes effect on November 20, promulgated in May and missed by the October post, and a staffing number from the National Assembly's annual audit. Everything below is as of November 6, 2026. The background is in [our baseline post on Korea's SMR program](/en/posts/korean-smr-ismr-status/).

## 1. The review clock: no new date

The i-SMR is Korea's domestic small modular reactor design, and its Standard Design Approval (SDA) application sits with NSSC (Nuclear Safety and Security Commission, Korea's regulator). The next dated steps have not moved.

Supplemented documents are due in June 2027 and main test results in June 2028. I found no announcement from NSSC or the applicant changing them.

## 3. Off the list: pre-review starts November 20

The legal basis is new Article 100-2 of the Nuclear Safety Act, added by Act No. 21679, promulgated May 19, 2026. The Act as a whole takes effect on January 1, 2027, but its addenda bring this article forward to six months after promulgation, and Korea's legislative notice portal gives that date as November 20. The enforcement rule cleared the Ministry of Government Legislation's review on November 4 and, per the same page, awaits promulgation.

It lets anyone who intends to apply for an SDA or a construction permit get a design looked at before filing.

- **Who it is for:** new reactors whose safety demonstration differs from already licensed designs, among others.
- **What gets reviewed:** design concept, main design features, safety demonstration methods and key safety functions.
- **What you file:** an application, a business plan and a pre-review request, among other documents.

Does this speed up the i-SMR? I read it as no, at least for the SDA review already under way. The i-SMR already filed on February 27 and went through a pre-application design review under an agreement from October 2023 to February 2026, according to Korean outlet Solution News (May 12). The addenda contain no transition clause for this article. The text would not rule out a pre-review before a later construction permit application, and I could not find an NSSC statement on the i-SMR's relation to it, so treat this as my reading of the structure.

The likelier first users are later designs. An NSSC division head told reporters on July 15 that about three companies had signaled interest, according to Energy Economic News (July 16), and I found no official confirmation of their names. This is also separate from NSSC's roadmap dates for SMR technical standards (2027) and further Act amendments (2028).

## 4. Off the list: seven people

At the October 13 audit in the National Assembly's science committee, a number came up that the licensing calendar does not show. Sankyung eNews (October 15) reported that SMR licensing and standards at NSSC rest with one temporary division of seven people. Lawmaker Kim Nam-guk said its term ends in December (Money Today).

Chair Choi Won-ho answered that at least a bureau-level unit of 33 is needed. Becoming a permanent division next year is expected to add five.

The seven do not include technical reviewers at KINS (Korea Institute of Nuclear Safety, the regulator's technical body), Asia Economy noted. KINS head Lim Seung-chul said about 100 of its staff work on SMRs and that around 180 more might be needed by 2035 (Financial News). Outlets differ on whether that figure covers SMR work or safety review as a whole. I have not checked these numbers against the official minutes.

As someone who studied this field, I had been measuring the 2028 margin only in the applicant's deadlines. The policy and licensing headcount runs on the same clock. I have not seen anything that links staffing to review duration, so this is a question and not a finding.

At the same hearing, a People Power Party lawmaker (outlets name different members) asked about a US-style manufacturing license, granted before factory build. Choi said it is under review and gave no date, according to Asia Economy (October 13). Under review is all it is.

## 5. The deliberation asks about shares, not SMR counts

The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment launched its nuclear deliberation committee on October 2 with chair Eun Jae-ho and eight members. That is nine, below the "up to 11" in September's plan. The schedule since:

- **October 21:** third meeting fixes an expert panel with three members each from environmental, nuclear and neutral backgrounds.
- **October 31 to November 14:** a baseline survey of 15,000 people, from which 300 citizen participants will be drawn by lot in proportion to population.
- **December 11 to 13:** a residential citizen deliberation.

Environmental groups declined to nominate experts, so the committee chose the environmental experts itself, according to eToday. Hankyoreh reported their objection: the demand forecast is inflated and new reactors are already decided before the process starts. The committee says the questionnaire will not ask for or against nuclear, but what range each power source's share should fall in, according to Metro Seoul.

That matters here. At its second meeting on October 14 the committee excluded projects with confirmed sites and the siting of any specific region from its discussion (MTN, October 15). I could not confirm whether the Gijang i-SMR, a candidate site, falls under that. The October 22 and November 1 reports I read do not list SMRs as a separate agenda item. A December recommendation (MTN, October 2) is unlikely to answer how many SMRs, or how large.

That question probably passes to the draft 12th Basic Plan for Long-term Electricity Supply and Demand. However the survey is framed, [the arguments on both sides](/en/posts/new-reactor-public-debate/) remain.

## 6. Not i-SMR: Doosan and TerraPower

- **The deal.** Doosan Enerbility said on August 14 it signed a manufacturing contract with TerraPower for its first Natrium plant at Kemmerer, Wyoming, a 345 MW unit (UPI). UPI lists a guard vessel, support structures and internal structures. World Nuclear News (August 21) lists the core barrel, guard vessel and internal supports. Neither gives a value, and I did not check the filing itself.
- **Why it is separate.** Natrium is a US sodium-cooled design and the customer is not KHNP. It has no bearing on the light-water i-SMR's licensing. [Our map of Korean builders in Western SMRs](/en/posts/korean-builders-western-smrs/) puts it among the overseas component deals.
- **KHNP abroad.** Korean outlet The Guru (October 1) reported a September SMR memorandum between KHNP and Thailand's GPSC, with reactor type and site undecided and no mention of the i-SMR. The memorandum and its timing rest on that report; Thai outlet Naewna (September 30) describes wider cooperation without a signing date. I found nothing new on the Norwegian cooperation since October.

## 7. What to watch in the next 30 to 60 days

1. **November 20.** Pre-review takes effect. Watch for the rule's promulgation and whether a first applicant is named.
2. **December.** The citizen session, the recommendation and the draft 12th Basic Plan, and how SMRs appear in them.
3. **Year-end.** Private members for the promotion committee, a date MSIT set for itself.
4. **The council.** October has passed. Does a first meeting get announced?
5. **NSSC.** SMR items at November and December meetings, and the size of next year's staffing increase.

**Where this goes next.** The review itself was quiet this month, and the only dated design-review step I know of is still June 2027. Until then, this tracker will mostly report on the rules, people and politics around the review.

**Industry note.** Doosan Enerbility (KRX: 034020) appears here for an overseas component contract. Korean companies' i-SMR work depends on a construction permit whose filing is reported for about 2029, and I found no announcement this month that moved it.
