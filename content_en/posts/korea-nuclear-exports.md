Sixteen months. That is how long the European Commission spent reviewing the Dukovany contract before deciding, on June 5, 2026, not to open an in-depth investigation into Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power (KHNP) under the EU's Foreign Subsidies Regulation. The decision left Korea's second nuclear export where its first, Barakah, stood a decade and a half earlier: contracted, financed and waiting for concrete.

Between those two projects sits a longer list of countries that were in the pipeline in 2023 and are not in it now. This article sorts every Korean nuclear export by stage, from talks to operation, and explains what each stage does and does not mean. The company structure behind the exports is in [our map of Korea's nuclear industry](/en/posts/korea-nuclear-industry-map/); here the focus is the projects. Unless noted, figures and stages are as of September 5, 2026; Dukovany developments from October and November 2026 are current to November 13.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Korea has one completed export (Barakah, four APR1400 units, all in commercial operation since September 2024) and one signed contract (Dukovany 5 and 6, two APR1000 units, CZK 407 billion, about $18.6 billion). On October 29, 2026 KHNP ended construction-contract talks with its Daewoo E&C–Doosan Enerbility consortium and plans to pick a new builder by July 2027; on November 3 it said the 2029 construction start is unchanged.
> - The January 2025 settlement with Westinghouse ended a two-year intellectual-property dispute but, according to reports the parties have not confirmed, keeps KHNP out of North America, the EU (except Czechia), the UK and Japan. Between late 2024 and August 2025 KHNP left Sweden, Slovenia, the Netherlands and Poland, a list that matches the reported split.
> - The live pipeline as of September 2026 is Saudi Arabia (a tender with no award), Vietnam (a partner selection announced for the third quarter of 2026), Turkey and the Philippines (memoranda only), plus SMR study agreements. None is a contract.

## 1. The export model: who signs, who builds

Korea exports a plant as a package the industry calls "Team Korea." One prime contractor signs with the customer, and the work is divided among the state-owned KEPCO group and private partners: KEPCO Engineering & Construction (KEPCO E&C) for design, Doosan Enerbility (Korea's reactor-vessel manufacturer) for the reactor vessel, steam generators and turbine, builders such as Hyundai E&C, Samsung C&T or Daewoo E&C for construction, KEPCO Nuclear Fuel for fuel and KEPCO KPS for commissioning and maintenance.

The prime contractor has changed from project to project. At Barakah it was KEPCO, the national utility; at Dukovany it is KHNP, KEPCO's subsidiary that operates all of Korea's reactors. In May 2026 the government set a rule for future bids: KEPCO leads external negotiations for every country, KHNP handles construction and operation under joint prime contracts, and a Nuclear Export Promotion Act to formalize this was planned for 2026 (Seoul Economic Daily, May 14, 2026).

The product is the **APR1400**, a 1,400 MW-class pressurized water reactor, and its 1,000 MW-class derivative for European grids, the **APR1000**.

- **US NRC.** The APR1400 received a standard design approval on September 28, 2018 and a design certification whose final rule took effect on September 19, 2019, originally valid for 15 years and extended to 40 years (to 2059) by an NRC rule change in 2025. A US site license would still be separate.
- **European Utility Requirements (EUR).** The European version, the EU-APR, was certified as EUR-compliant in October 2017 (World Nuclear News, October 9, 2017); the APR1000 followed in March 2023 (announced March 2). EUR is a utility-side specification, not a license.

## 2. Barakah 1–4: the reference plant

The Emirates Nuclear Energy Corporation (ENEC) awarded the Barakah contract to a KEPCO-led consortium on December 27, 2009, at a reported 2009 contract value of about $20.4 billion for four APR1400 units; no final cost has been published. It was Korea's first nuclear export, won against a French consortium of Areva, GDF Suez and Total, and GE-Hitachi.

| Unit | Construction start | Grid connection | Commercial operation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit 1 | July 2012 | August 2020 | April 2021 |
| Unit 2 | April 2013 | September 2021 | March 2022 |
| Unit 3 | September 2014 | October 2022 | February 2023 |
| Unit 4 | July 2015 | March 2024 | September 5, 2024 |

Sources for the table: ENEC and the World Nuclear Association (page updated July 16, 2026).

The 2009 plan had unit 1 operating in 2017, so the first unit came roughly four years late, concentrated in commissioning and operator licensing. But the four units arrived roughly a year apart, which is the "repeat build" pattern Korean bidders point to, and the 5.6 GW plant now supplies about a quarter of the UAE's electricity, according to ENEC.

**KEPCO's stake did not end at handover.** In 2016 ENEC and KEPCO formed two joint ventures, Barakah One, which owns the plant and sells its power, and Nawah Energy, which operates it; KEPCO holds 18 percent of each. In June 2019 Nawah signed a long-term maintenance services agreement with KHNP, supported by KEPCO KPS.

**Expansion talks are not a project.** Talks on two more units were reported in August 2023, but the World Nuclear Association's UAE profile, as of July 2026, records no concrete progress. What exists is two memoranda signed on November 18, 2025 during a UAE–Korea summit, one between KEPCO and ENEC and one between their subsidiaries, covering SMRs, AI for plant operations and joint projects in third countries; the first such attempt is Vietnam (section 5).

## 3. Dukovany 5 and 6: the contract that survived three challenges

The Czech project is a state-led new build at the existing Dukovany site, about 150 km southeast of Prague. The owner, Elektrárna Dukovany II (EDU II), is 80 percent held by the state and 20 percent by the utility ČEZ. The tender, launched in 2022, was changed on January 31, 2024 to binding offers for up to four units; Westinghouse was excluded then for not submitting a binding offer, leaving KHNP and EDF.

The contract covers two APR1000 units of about 1,050 MW each for CZK 407 billion (about $18.6 billion), with electricity priced below 90 euros per megawatt-hour and roughly 60 percent of the work going to Czech companies. KHNP's statement on the subsidies decision said it had "not received any subsidies that could damage or distort fair competition" in relation to the project.

**The three challenges.** EDF argued that KHNP's fixed-price guarantee was unfeasible without illegal state support; the Czech competition authority, two courts and the Commission's preliminary review each declined to act on that claim. Westinghouse's objection, that the APR1000 uses its licensed technology, was overtaken by the January 2025 settlement (section 4). Still open is the state-aid case for the second unit: the Commission approved aid for one unit in April 2024, Czechia notified an expanded two-unit support package (a state loan of EUR 23–30 billion plus a 40-year contract for difference) in October 2025, and the Commission opened an in-depth investigation in December 2025. The ministry expects approval around the first quarter of 2027.

**The Temelín option.** The July 2024 decision included an option for two more units at ČEZ's Temelín site. As of September 2026 it has not been exercised; Czech ministry statements in 2026 say the units "are also being considered," and ČEZ has separately contracted Rolls-Royce SMR for early works on small reactors at Temelín.

### October–November 2026: the construction talks collapse, the first tenders open

The first visible crack inside Team Korea came after the contract, not before it. On October 29, 2026 KHNP said it had ended negotiations with the Daewoo E&C–Doosan Enerbility consortium on the Dukovany construction subcontract, after working-level talks, a dispute mediation committee and executive talks failed (World Nuclear News, October 29). It began terminating the joint cooperation agreement and preparing a tender for a new contractor.

The dispute was over who pays when quantities grow.

- **KHNP's account.** It proposed a lump-sum price fixed on basic-design baseline quantities (World Nuclear News), and its business division head Choi Il-kyung said the consortium's extra demand exceeded KRW 1 trillion (about $740 million), more than KHNP could absorb (Yonhap, October 29).
- **The consortium's account.** The consortium wanted payment adjusted to detailed-design quantities (World Nuclear News) and said KHNP changed the terms on October 7, then refused its request to extend talks to December 31 (SBS Biz and Yonhap, October 29).
- **Main equipment is separate.** Doosan Enerbility said its reactor-equipment and turbine-generator contracts with KHNP, signed on December 16, 2025, remain in effect without amendment, and that the consortium-branded press release had been issued by Daewoo without consulting it (World Nuclear News and SBS Biz, October 29).

KHNP plans a bidder briefing in January 2027, a tender in April and a choice by July (World Nuclear News). KHNP said it will not contract the construction work to a foreign company, will do itself whatever it can, and framed the choice as one among domestic groups such as the Daewoo-Doosan, Hyundai and Samsung consortia (Yonhap, October 29). Doosan said it will bid again, since KHNP has said existing members may take part.

**Seoul defended the project and criticized KHNP.** Kim Jung-kwan, Minister of Trade, Industry and Resources, wrote on social media on October 29 that Korea would complete the Czech project without wavering and that he respected KHNP's judgment that a new contractor was unavoidable; on November 1 he posted on X that the Team Korea dispute was painful and urged KHNP, as prime contractor, to show leadership and listen to its partners (Yonhap; E-Today and oEnergetice, November 1). At a National Assembly committee on November 9 he said the project must not fail, and KHNP president Kim Hoe-cheon said the schedule still looked achievable working back from the target date (Yonhap, November 9).

Prague stayed calm. Czech Industry and Trade Minister Karel Havlíček relayed his Korean counterpart's assurance that construction and its schedule were not at risk at this point (oEnergetice and ČTK, October 29). On November 3 KHNP told the Czech news agency ČTK that the agreed schedule, including the 2029 construction start, remains unchanged, while ČEZ chief executive Daniel Beneš noted that the contract's price and schedule are fixed and missing them brings penalties (ČTK, November 3).

The dates differ by source. The Czech plans published so far put construction start in 2029 and completion of the first unit in 2036 (ČTK), while World Nuclear News on October 29 gave site preparation from January 2028, full construction from November 2029 and completion of the two units in 2039 and 2040. We could not confirm which is the current baseline as of November 13.

Site work kept moving.

- **Cooling towers.** On October 26 KHNP opened a restricted tender for the design, technology supply and construction of hybrid wet-dry cooling towers, which cut water withdrawals; two consortia, both including Czech firms, are eligible, and a bidder briefing was planned for November (oEnergetice, October 26).
- **Auxiliary equipment.** Reporting on KHNP's supplier seminar in Prague on September 16, oEnergetice said more than 60 Czech firms had met KHNP's supplier qualification requirements and that KHNP planned its first auxiliary equipment and materials tender for November (oEnergetice, September 16). We had not found a report of that tender being issued as of November 13.

As someone who studied this field, I read the fight as the first time the question raised in section 6, whether a first APR1000 can be built inside the contract price, has shown up as a number. It is not an answer yet.

## 4. The Westinghouse settlement: what is known and what is not

The APR1400's lineage runs through the System 80+ design of Combustion Engineering, whose nuclear business passed to ABB in 1990 and to Westinghouse in 2000. In October 2022 Westinghouse sued KEPCO and KHNP in a US federal court, arguing that the design contained Westinghouse-licensed technology and that exporting it required its consent. The suit hung over the Czech bid for two years.

On January 16, 2025 Westinghouse announced a "global settlement agreement" with KEPCO and KHNP: terms confidential, all legal actions to be dismissed, and a statement that the settlement "sets the stage for future cooperation between the parties to advance new nuclear projects globally." That is the whole public record from the parties themselves.

**What has been reported, not confirmed.** In August 2025 The Korea Herald and The Korea Times, citing unnamed industry sources, described terms that neither company has confirmed:

- KHNP may bid in the Philippines, Vietnam, Kazakhstan, Morocco, Egypt, Brazil, Argentina, Jordan, Turkey, the UAE, Saudi Arabia and South Africa, plus Czechia, but not in North America, the UK, Japan, Ukraine or other EU countries.
- For each reactor exported, the Korean side would buy about $650 million of Westinghouse goods and services and pay about $175 million in licensing fees, over a 50-year term.
- Korean SMRs would need a Westinghouse "technology independence verification" before export.

KHNP's president at the time, Whang Joo-ho, declined to confirm specifics but said that, as the party accepting the terms, he could not call them fair, while adding that the company could still profit under them. The presidential office ordered the trade ministry to examine whether the deal had a proper legal basis and followed due process; no result had been published in the sources we checked as of September 2026, and Whang offered to resign in September 2025 amid the controversy. A KHNP–Westinghouse joint venture for the US market was discussed in August 2025, and on August 24, 2026 The Korea Economic Daily reported that Seoul was reviewing a US proposal for a joint equity stake in Westinghouse, a report the government denied the next day, saying Westinghouse is not part of the Korea–US strategic investment program. Neither had produced a signed agreement as of early September.

The reported market split matches what KHNP did next: it withdrew from Vattenfall's selection in Sweden at the end of 2024, from Slovenia's JEK2 in January 2025, from the Dutch Borssele project on March 17, 2025 and from Poland's Pątnów project in August 2025. KHNP cited business priorities, the Czech project and SMRs.

Notes on the live entries:

- **Saudi Arabia.** The Duwaiheen tender for two reactors of about 1.4 GW each was issued in 2022; the bid deadline slipped from December 2022 to July 2024, and the World Nuclear Association's profile, updated July 24, 2026, still lists the four vendors as approved to bid with no award, so it is a tender without a public award. The US–Saudi civil nuclear cooperation ("123") agreement was signed in July 2026 according to the same profile; Korean vendors would need it for US-origin components. Separately, KAERI's 100 MW SMART reactor is being pursued with Saudi Arabia's KA-CARE.
- **Vietnam.** Ninh Thuan 1 went to Russia in March 2026; Japan withdrew from Ninh Thuan 2 in January 2026 citing the schedule. KEPCO signed a workforce memorandum with PetroVietnam in August 2025 and, during President Lee Jae-myung's April 2026 state visit, an agreement with Korea Eximbank and Korea Trade Insurance to explore Korean financing. In July 2026 Vietnam's industry ministry said a partner would be chosen in the third quarter; Seoul Economic Daily reported on July 31 that Korea would bid together with the UAE for a four-unit project the paper valued at KRW 12–13 trillion (about $9–10 billion). No selection had been announced in the sources we checked as of September 5.
- **Turkey.** KEPCO proposed four APR1400 units at Sinop in January 2023 at Turkey's request. The KEPCO–TUNAS memorandum of November 24, 2025 covers data sharing, site evaluation, financing and training but names no site or reactor; Turkey had not announced a Sinop vendor as of September 2026.
- **Philippines.** The Meralco–KHNP–KEXIM memorandum covers early-stage development, site selection, training and a review of Korean financing, toward a Philippine goal of first nuclear power by 2032. It specifies no reactor.
- **SMR agreements.** KHNP has signed SMR study memoranda with Thailand's EGAT (June 2025) and Singapore's Energy Market Authority (March 1, 2026), among others. Korea's i-SMR filed for Standard Design Authorization on February 27, 2026 and has no approved design to sell yet; see [our i-SMR status report](/en/posts/korean-smr-ismr-status/) and [the global SMR licensing map](/en/posts/smr-global-status-2026/).

## 6. What the record shows

### Strengths: one delivered plant, one contract price still standing

By our count, since 2010 Barakah is the only large-reactor export completed in a newcomer country by a vendor from outside China and Russia. It is also one plant, for one customer that financed most of it, built by companies that were constructing the same design at home in parallel. The same team is now contracted in Czechia, where the government says the tender was decided on price and delivery guarantees; the legal and subsidy reviews since then have left the contract price in place, but they tested its legality, not whether a first-of-a-kind APR1000 can be built for it.

### Open issues: terms, money, rules and home policy

- **Westinghouse terms.** If the reported terms are accurate, the addressable market is a named list of countries and each exported unit carries a fixed payment to Westinghouse. The government's review has not been made public, and any arrangement that would reopen the US market remained unconfirmed as of early September. As of October 1, 2026, a non-binding US-Korea framework contemplates a waiver that would allow up to two APR1400s in the US, financed through Korea's investment fund, and a Korean minority stake in Westinghouse reported at 5 to 10 percent; no site, owner or contract has been confirmed (see [our report on the package](/en/posts/us-korea-nuclear-package/)).
- **Financing.** Barakah was financed mainly by the Abu Dhabi government, with a Korea Eximbank loan alongside; Dukovany is financed by a Czech state loan to EDU II. The next buyers are asking vendors to bring money: Vietnam's stated criteria include concessional credit below 3 percent interest and 30 to 60 percent local content, and the one reported answer so far is to pair with UAE capital (Seoul Economic Daily, July 31, 2026).
- **EU rules.** The foreign-subsidies review passed at the preliminary stage, but the state-aid decision for Dukovany 6 is expected in early 2027, and any future EU bid would face both regimes from the start.
- **First-of-a-kind design.** No APR1000 has been built anywhere; Dukovany 5 will be the first, under a fixed price, so Barakah's repeat-build record applies to the team, not to the unit. The October 2026 construction dispute was about exactly that: who carries quantity growth inside the fixed price.
- **Domestic policy.** Korea's own program is two large units and one SMR under the 11th Basic Plan, with sites chosen in June 2026, and a public deliberation this autumn on whether to add more; see [our report on the September 2026 deliberation](/en/posts/new-reactor-public-debate/).

### How to read the next headline

Three checks sort most export news.

- **Which reactor.** APR1400 (UAE, the Turkey proposal), APR1000 (Czechia; APR1000/1400 were offered to Kazakhstan) or an SMR with no approved design yet.
- **Who is prime contractor.** KEPCO, KHNP or, from May 2026, both under a joint contract.
- **What stage.** A memorandum is talks, a bid is a tender, and only a signed construction contract with financing behind it counts as a project.


## Where this goes next

As of September 2026, three dates were set to decide what came next: Vietnam's partner choice for Ninh Thuan 2, announced for the third quarter; the Nuclear Export Promotion Act and any KEPCO–Westinghouse arrangement, which together set who bids and where; and the Czech state-aid decision on Dukovany 6, expected in early 2027. Since November a fourth sits beside them: the new Dukovany construction contractor, due by July 2027, and whether the 2029 start holds after it. Saudi Arabia's tender has slipped repeatedly since 2022; treat headlines about it with caution until a contract is signed.

My own filter for this pipeline is the three-question test above: which reactor, who is prime contractor, what stage. Everything else is a press release.
