EnergyNote is written by a nuclear and radiation professional working in South Korea. The Korean edition covers everyday radiation questions and Korea's energy debates; this English edition focuses on what an insider's view from Korea can add for international readers.

## What you will find here

- **SMR and nuclear licensing, tracked by stage** — design approval, construction permit, construction, commissioning — rather than by press release.
- **Korea's nuclear program** — the i-SMR design review, SMART100, Korea's new-build debate, decommissioning of Kori-1, and the Korean manufacturers inside Western SMR supply chains.
- **Fukushima treated-water discharge as seen from next door** — how Korea monitors its own waters and seafood, and what the numbers say three years in.
- **Radiation in daily life**, when there is something specific to add.

## Principles

1. **Numbers carry their dates.** Every article shows a "Data as of" date under the title, and time-sensitive figures are dated inline. When a figure is updated, the date is updated with it.
2. **Primary sources first.** Regulators (NRC, CNSC, ONR, Korea's NSSC and KINS), the IAEA, UNSCEAR and ICRP, peer-reviewed papers, and company filings. Where only news reporting is available, the outlet is named.
3. **Both sides of contested questions.** New nuclear build, Fukushima discharge and low-dose radiation are argued on the merits, with the counter-arguments stated in their strongest form.
4. **No hype.** SMRs are one of several candidate technologies, not a settled future. Company targets are reported as targets.
